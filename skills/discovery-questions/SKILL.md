# Discovery Question Generator

> **Part of the [Product Marketing Toolkit](../../README.md).** Before you start, read whatever exists in `context/` (ICP/personas, objections, and competitors). If those files are empty or missing, ask for the essentials instead of guessing. Label anything you infer as an assumption, and never invent statistics, customer names, or quotes: use a bracketed placeholder like `[sourced metric, n, date]` instead.

A systematic approach to building discovery questions that uncover customer pain points, build urgency, and guide prospects toward your solution.

## Overview

This skill helps product marketers create discovery questions that:
- Uncover current state and existing approaches
- Surface pain points and challenges
- Amplify the cost of inaction
- Build desire for solutions
- Expose competitor weaknesses
- Qualify opportunities effectively

## Core Principles

**Open-Ended Questions**
Ask questions that require explanation, not yes/no answers. Get prospects talking.

**Listen More Than Talk**
Discovery is about uncovering, not pitching. Ask, listen, probe deeper.

**Build on Responses**
Use answers to inform next questions. Follow the thread of conversation.

**Quantify Impact**
Move from qualitative pain to quantified business impact whenever possible.

**Customer Language**
Frame questions in terms prospects use, not your product terminology.

## SPIN Question Framework

Based on SPIN selling methodology, adapted for SaaS product marketing.

### Situation Questions

**Purpose:** Understand current state and context

**What They Do:**
- Establish baseline understanding
- Gather facts about current processes
- Identify tools and systems in use
- Map organizational structure
- Understand constraints and requirements

**When to Use:**
- Early in discovery
- When you need context
- To establish credibility
- Before identifying problems

**Structure:**
```
"Walk me through [current process/approach]..."
"How do you currently [task/workflow]..."
"What [tools/systems] are you using for [function]..."
"Who is involved in [process/decision]..."
```

**Examples:**
- "Walk me through how you handle [the job] today, start to finish?"
- "What tools are you using today for [function]?"
- "Who else touches this process?"
- "How does this work across different teams, brands, or product lines?"
- "What does your team's workflow look like for creating campaigns?"

**Best Practices:**
- Don't overdo situation questions - 2-3 max
- Use them to set context, not interrogate
- Move to problem questions quickly
- Avoid asking what you could research beforehand

### Problem Questions

**Purpose:** Surface pain points and challenges

**What They Do:**
- Identify dissatisfaction with current state
- Uncover specific challenges
- Reveal inefficiencies or gaps
- Surface unmet needs
- Create awareness of problems

**When to Use:**
- After establishing current state
- When prospect describes their approach
- To dig into mentioned challenges
- Before quantifying impact

**Structure:**
```
"What challenges do you face with [current approach]..."
"What happens when [problematic scenario]..."
"How much [time/effort/cost] does [process] require..."
"What prevents you from [desired outcome]..."
```

**Examples:**
- "What challenges do you face with your current setup for [job to be done]?"
- "What happens when you need to send a time-sensitive message?"
- "How much time does your team spend switching between platforms?"
- "What prevents you from personalizing messages effectively?"
- "How predictable are your costs month-to-month with your current provider?"

**Best Practices:**
- Ask about specific, not general, problems
- Follow their cues - dig where they show concern
- Don't lead with your solution's benefits
- Listen for emotion - that's where real pain lives

### Implication Questions

**Purpose:** Amplify the cost of inaction and business impact

**What They Do:**
- Quantify the impact of problems
- Connect problems to business outcomes
- Build urgency to solve
- Make consequences clear
- Elevate priority

**When to Use:**
- After identifying problems
- To build urgency
- When stakes seem low
- Before introducing solutions

**Structure:**
```
"What's the impact of [problem] on [business outcome]..."
"How does [issue] affect your team's ability to [goal]..."
"What would it mean if you could solve [pain point]..."
"What does [problem] cost you in [time/money/opportunity]..."
```

**Examples:**
- "What's the impact of messages getting buried in inboxes on your revenue?"
- "How does switching between tools affect your team's productivity?"
- "What does the lack of unified reporting cost you in decision-making time?"
- "What would surprise price increases mean for your marketing budget?"
- "How does poor deliverability affect your customer relationships?"

**Best Practices:**
- Connect problems to money, time, or growth
- Help prospects quantify impact themselves
- Don't exaggerate - stay credible
- Multiple small impacts can compound to big problems

### Need-Payoff Questions

**Purpose:** Build desire for solutions and outcomes

**What They Do:**
- Get prospect to articulate value
- Create vision of better state
- Shift focus to benefits
- Build commitment to change
- Make the prospect sell themselves

**When to Use:**
- After amplifying problems
- Before pitching solution
- To build buying vision
- When closing commitment

**Structure:**
```
"If you could [solve problem], what would that enable..."
"How valuable would it be to [achieve outcome]..."
"What would [capability] mean for your business..."
"How would your team benefit from [solution attribute]..."
```

**Examples:**
- "If [the core problem] went away, what would that mean for your [goal]?"
- "How valuable would it be to see all your marketing performance in one place?"
- "What would having all of this in one place enable for you?"
- "If you could eliminate swivel-chair work between tools, how would that change your workflow?"
- "How would predictable pricing affect your planning?"

**Best Practices:**
- Let them paint the vision, not you
- Connect to earlier stated problems
- Build on their language and priorities
- Get them excited about possibilities

### Trap-Setting Questions

**Purpose:** Expose competitor or current solution weaknesses

**What They Do:**
- Surface dissatisfaction with current approach
- Reveal competitor limitations
- Create doubt about status quo
- Position your differentiation
- Set up competitive positioning

**When to Use:**
- In competitive situations
- When they mention current provider
- To surface switching triggers
- Before positioning your solution

**Structure:**
```
"How [easy/difficult] is it to [thing your product does better]..."
"Do you find yourself [pain point your competitor causes]..."
"How [satisfied/frustrated] are you with [competitor weakness]..."
"What would you change about [current solution] if you could..."
```

**Examples:**
- "How easy is it to see whether [current solution] is actually working?"
- "Do you find yourself juggling multiple logins and platforms?"
- "How responsive is your current provider when you need support?"
- "What would you change about your current pricing model if you could?"
- "How much do you actually use all the features you're paying for?"

**Best Practices:**
- Frame neutrally, not leading
- Listen for frustration or resignation
- Don't bash competitors explicitly
- Plant seeds, don't hammer
- Follow up based on their reaction

## Discovery by Customer Segment

> **Worked example.** The segment questions below were written for a customer-messaging product (reminders, notifications, promotions). Use them as a pattern: swap in the jobs your product does for each segment you sell to.

### E-Commerce Businesses

**Situation Questions:**
- "What's your current cart abandonment rate?"
- "How do you currently notify customers about flash sales?"
- "Walk me through your order confirmation and shipping notification process?"

**Problem Questions:**
- "What challenges do you face recovering abandoned carts?"
- "How quickly can customers typically respond to your time-sensitive offers?"
- "What happens when customers have questions about their orders?"

**Implication Questions:**
- "What does a 1% improvement in cart recovery mean for your revenue?"
- "How much revenue do you lose when flash sales don't reach customers in time?"
- "What's the cost of customers calling support instead of self-serving?"

**Need-Payoff Questions:**
- "If you could automatically reach customers who abandon carts within minutes, what would that mean for recovery rates?"
- "How valuable would instant notification for flash sales be?"
- "What would proactive order updates mean for customer experience?"

### Professional Services

**Situation Questions:**
- "How do you currently remind clients about appointments?"
- "What's your process for confirming bookings?"
- "How do you handle last-minute scheduling changes?"

**Problem Questions:**
- "What's your no-show rate?"
- "How much time does your team spend on appointment reminders?"
- "What challenges do you face with last-minute cancellations?"

**Implication Questions:**
- "What does each no-show cost you in lost revenue?"
- "How does administrative work take away from billable hours?"
- "What's the impact of unfilled appointment slots on your business?"

**Need-Payoff Questions:**
- "If you could reduce no-shows by 30%, what would that mean for revenue?"
- "How would automated reminders free up your team's time?"
- "What would better-filled schedules mean for your capacity?"

### Multi-Brand / Multi-Audience

**Situation Questions:**
- "How are you currently managing messaging for different brands?"
- "What does your process look like for keeping brand communications separate?"
- "How do you ensure the right message goes to the right audience?"

**Problem Questions:**
- "Have you ever accidentally sent a promotion for Brand A to Brand B subscribers?"
- "How much time does managing multiple accounts or platforms take?"
- "What challenges do you face with fragmented data across brands?"

**Implication Questions:**
- "What's the cost of accidental cross-promotion to brand trust?"
- "How does tool fragmentation affect your team's efficiency?"
- "What does lack of unified reporting mean for strategic decisions?"

**Need-Payoff Questions:**
- "If you could manage all brands from one platform with proper separation, how would that change operations?"
- "How valuable would unified reporting across all your brands be?"
- "What would a single source of truth for all brand data enable?"

## Discovery by Pain Point Category

### Cost/Budget Pain Points

**Questions:**
- "Walk me through your monthly bill from [current provider]?"
- "How predictable are your costs month-to-month?"
- "What was your experience with their last price increase?"
- "How much do you spend across all your marketing tools combined?"
- "What's your total cost of ownership including [hidden costs]?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "How does that impact your ability to plan budgets?"
- "If costs were 20% lower with the same results, what would you do with those savings?"

### Complexity/Usability Pain Points

**Questions:**
- "How long did it take to get [feature/workflow] set up?"
- "How often does your team need support to accomplish tasks?"
- "What percentage of features do you actually use?"
- "How much training did new team members need?"
- "What's the learning curve been like?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "How does this complexity affect adoption and usage?"
- "What would simplicity mean for your team's productivity?"

### Integration/Workflow Pain Points

**Questions:**
- "How many different tools are you logging into daily?"
- "How do you currently sync data between systems?"
- "Where do you go to get a complete view of customer interactions?"
- "How much time does your team spend on manual data work?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "What's the cost of disconnected systems?"
- "How would a unified platform change your workflow?"

### Support/Service Pain Points

**Questions:**
- "When was the last time you needed support - what was that experience like?"
- "How quickly can you typically get help when you need it?"
- "Do you have a dedicated contact or account team?"
- "How satisfied are you with the support quality?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "How does support response time affect your business?"
- "What would dedicated human support mean for your success?"

### Performance/Results Pain Points

**Questions:**
- "How are your current engagement rates trending?"
- "What ROI are you seeing from [channel/tool]?"
- "How do your results compare to your goals?"
- "Which metrics matter most to you?"

**Follow-ups:**
- "What would a 2x improvement in [metric] mean for your business?"
- "How much more valuable would proven results be?"

## Question Sequencing Strategies

### The Funnel Approach

**Start Broad → Narrow Down**
1. Situation: "How do you manage marketing today?"
2. Problem: "What challenges do you face?"
3. Probe Specific: "Tell me more about [mentioned challenge]"
4. Implication: "What does that cost you?"
5. Need-Payoff: "What would solving that enable?"

### The Pain Ladder

**Surface → Amplify → Visualize Solution**
1. Problem: "What challenges do you face?"
2. Implication: "How does that affect your business?"
3. Quantify: "What does that cost in [time/money]?"
4. Need-Payoff: "What would solving this mean?"
5. Vision: "How would that change your approach?"

### The Competitive Setup

**Current → Dissatisfaction → Alternative**
1. Situation: "What are you using today?"
2. Satisfaction Probe: "What's working well?"
3. Dissatisfaction: "What would you change?"
4. Implication: "How does that hold you back?"
5. Need-Payoff: "What would a better approach enable?"

## Building Question Libraries

### By Product/Feature

For each major product or feature, create:
- 3-5 Situation questions
- 5-7 Problem questions
- 3-5 Implication questions
- 3-5 Need-Payoff questions
- 3-5 Trap-Setting questions (competitive)

### By Persona

Adapt questions for:
- **Technical Buyers:** Focus on integration, security, capabilities
- **Economic Buyers:** Focus on ROI, cost, business impact
- **End Users:** Focus on usability, workflow, day-to-day experience
- **Champions:** Focus on organizational benefits, change management

### By Stage

**Early Stage (Qualification):**
- Mostly Situation and Problem questions
- Light Implication questions
- Goal: Understand fit and priority

**Mid Stage (Building Urgency):**
- Heavy Implication questions
- Some Need-Payoff questions
- Goal: Amplify pain and build desire

**Late Stage (Closing):**
- Need-Payoff questions
- Confirm value
- Goal: Secure commitment

## Best Practices

**Don't Interrogate**
Discovery is a conversation, not an interview. Make it natural.

**Follow Their Lead**
If they mention something interesting, go deeper. Don't rigidly follow your list.

**One Question at a Time**
Multiple questions in one confuses and overwhelms.

**Pause for Answers**
Give them time to think. Silence is okay.

**Confirm Understanding**
"So what I'm hearing is..." shows you listen and clarifies.

**Take Notes**
Capture key points, pain indicators, and quotes to use later.

**Adapt in Real-Time**
If a question lands well, ask more like it. If it falls flat, pivot.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Leading Questions**
❌ "Don't you find it frustrating that..."
✅ "What's your experience with..."

**Solution-Centric Questions**
❌ "How would you like to manage everything in one platform?"
✅ "How do you currently manage your different tools?"

**Too Many Situation Questions**
❌ Spending 20 minutes on background
✅ 2-3 situation questions, then move to problems

**Asking What You Should Know**
❌ "What does your company do?"
✅ Research beforehand, ask smart questions

**Binary Questions**
❌ "Do you have problems with X?" (Yes/No)
✅ "What challenges do you face with X?" (Explanation)

**Not Listening**
❌ Asking next question while they're answering
✅ Actively listen, probe based on responses

## Integration with Sales Process

**Discovery Feeds:**
- **Qualification:** Is this a good fit?
- **Positioning:** Which messages will resonate?
- **Objection Handling:** What concerns will arise?
- **Proof Points:** What data do they need?
- **Close:** What's driving their decision?

**Document and Share:**
- Key pain points uncovered
- Quantified business impact
- Decision criteria
- Timeline and urgency
- Competitors in consideration

## Enablement Best Practices

**Train Sales Teams:**
- Role-play discovery conversations
- Share recordings of great discovery
- Provide question libraries
- Give talk tracks for each question type
- Show how answers connect to positioning

**Make Questions Accessible:**
- Include in CRM for easy access
- Build into pitch decks
- Create quick-reference guides
- Organize by persona/segment
- Update based on what works

**Measure Effectiveness:**
- Which questions surface pain most effectively?
- Which questions lead to closed deals?
- Where do reps get stuck?
- What objections could have been prevented?
