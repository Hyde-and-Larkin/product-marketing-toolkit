# Positioning review rubric

Score each dimension 1-5. Quote the line from the artifact that justifies the score.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 | A buyer would get it in one read and could repeat it to a colleague |
| 4 | Clear, with one soft spot |
| 3 | Understandable if the reader already knows the category |
| 2 | Generic; could belong to several competitors |
| 1 | Missing, or actively confusing |

## 1. Customer clarity

Is it obvious who this is for?

- 5: Names a specific role, company type, or situation ("finance teams closing the books at 50-500 person companies")
- 3: Names a broad group ("teams," "businesses," "marketers")
- 1: No customer at all, or "everyone"

Check: could a reader self-select in or out within one sentence?

## 2. Pain

Is the problem concrete and felt?

- 5: Describes the problem in the words a customer would use, with a consequence ("month-end close takes a week, so leadership sees last month's numbers in the middle of this month")
- 3: Names a category of problem ("inefficient workflows")
- 1: Starts from the product, not the problem

## 3. Differentiation

Why this and not the alternative? The alternative includes competitors, spreadsheets, an agency, a new hire, and doing nothing.

- 5: States a difference the competitor can't easily claim, and it fails the swap test (it would be false with a competitor's name in it)
- 3: Lists features that competitors also list
- 1: Category boilerplate ("powerful, easy-to-use, all-in-one")

## 4. Proof

Are the claims believable?

- 5: The main claim has a specific number, a named customer, or a demonstrable mechanism behind it
- 3: Logos or vague social proof without connection to the claim
- 1: Unsupported superlatives ("the best," "the leading," "10x")

## 5. Specificity

Could a reader picture the product working?

- 5: Concrete outcomes, timeframes, or examples ("weekly report in ten minutes")
- 3: Mix of concrete and abstract
- 1: Abstract nouns throughout ("unlock," "empower," "transform," "seamless")

## 6. Fit for the job

Does the artifact do what its format needs?

- Homepage: the first screen alone says what it is, who it's for, and why it's different
- Sales deck: the opening frames the customer's problem before the product shows up
- Launch post: says what changed, who should care, and what to do next
- Investor pitch: the market, the wedge, and why this team wins
- Positioning statement: every slot (for/who/our product is/that/unlike/we) is filled with something specific

## Common AI-copy failure patterns

Flag these when you see them. They're the usual signs of a first draft nobody reviewed.

- Headlines that describe a category instead of a customer
- "Whether you're X or Y" openings
- Three adjectives in a row
- Benefits with no subject ("Save time. Grow faster. Work smarter.")
- Claims that sound like stats but have no number
- Every section the same length and shape
