# Competitive Battlecard Creator

> **Part of the [Product Marketing Toolkit](../../README.md).** Before you start, read whatever exists in `context/` (competitors file and the latest competitive-intel brief). If those files are empty or missing, ask for the essentials instead of guessing. Label anything you infer as an assumption, and never invent statistics, customer names, or quotes: use a bracketed placeholder like `[sourced metric, n, date]` instead.

A systematic framework for building competitive battlecards that help sales teams win against specific competitors through clear differentiation and strategic positioning.

## Overview

This skill helps product marketers:
- Structure competitive intelligence for easy consumption
- Identify and articulate key differentiators
- Develop trap-setting questions to expose competitor weaknesses
- Create actionable battlecards for sales teams
- Build switcher-specific strategies
- Update competitive positioning based on win/loss insights

## Core Principles

**Evidence-Based**
Base all competitive intelligence on verified sources: win/loss interviews, sales feedback, customer research, and direct product evaluation.

**Seller-Focused**
Build battlecards for sales teams, not analysts. Make information actionable, scannable, and easy to use in live conversations.

**Honest Assessment**
Acknowledge where competitors are strong. Credibility comes from balanced analysis, not cheerleading.

**Continuously Updated**
Competitors ship features too. Battlecards must be living documents that evolve with the market.

**Differentiation, Not Comparison**
Focus on meaningful differences that matter to customers, not feature checklists.

## Battlecard Structure

### Quick Reference Section (Top of Card)

**At-a-Glance Comparison**
*Purpose:* Instant visual summary for pre-call prep

*Include:*
- Company overview (1-2 sentences)
- Pricing comparison (general ranges)
- Top 3 strengths (theirs)
- Top 3 strengths (yours)
- Key differentiators (3-5 bullets)

*Format:*
Table or side-by-side comparison for scannability

### When You'll Encounter Them

**Deal Scenarios**
- Types of deals where this competitor appears
- Deal size ranges
- Customer segments they target

**Customer Profiles**
- Industries or verticals where they're strong
- Company sizes they serve best
- Use cases where they excel

**Geographic Presence**
- Markets where they have strong presence
- Regions where you have advantage

*Example:*
"You'll encounter [Competitor] in mid-market e-commerce deals ($50K-$500K ARR), particularly in fashion and consumer goods verticals. They're strong in North America but weak in EMEA."

### Why We Win

**Our Specific Advantages** (3-5 points)
For each advantage, include:
- The differentiator (what we do)
- Why it matters (customer impact)
- Proof point (data, customer quote, or case study)

*Template:*
```
**[Differentiator]**
We: [Specific capability or approach]
They: [How competitor differs]
Impact: [Why this matters to customer]
Proof: [Data point or customer quote]
```

*Example:*
```
**Unified Platform**
We: One platform that holds the workflow and the customer data together
They: Point tool that needs a separate login and its own copy of the data
Impact: Less "swivel-chair" work between tools, and one place to see what's working
Proof: "If [Your Company] would have had it - we would have used it." - Customer quote from switcher
```

### How to Position Against Them

**Trap-Setting Questions**
Questions designed to surface dissatisfaction with the competitor

*Purpose:* Uncover pain points before you introduce your solution

*Structure:*
```
[Open-ended question that reveals competitor weakness]
→ [Follow-up that quantifies impact]
→ [Question that builds desire for alternative]
```

*Examples:*
- "How much time does your team spend moving work between [their tool] and the rest of your stack?"
- "How do you currently see whether [the job your product does] is actually working?"
- "What's your process when you need support for a technical issue?"
- "How predictable are your costs month-to-month?"

**Landmines** (Competitor Weaknesses)
Issues customers face with this competitor

*For Each Landmine:*
- The issue (what's wrong)
- Customer evidence (quotes from win/loss)
- Impact (how this hurts the business)

*Example:*
```
**Landmine: Confusing Pricing**
Issue: Monthly subscription fees PLUS per-message costs
Evidence: "The monthly fees on top of message costs made it impossible to budget"
Impact: Unpredictable costs, budget overruns, difficulty forecasting
```

**Our Approach** (How We Solve It)
How your solution addresses each landmine

*For Each:*
- Our solution (specific capability)
- Customer benefit (outcome)
- Proof (metric or quote)

*Example:*
```
**Our Approach: Transparent Pricing**
Solution: One predictable price with no usage overages
Benefit: Easy budgeting and cost forecasting
Proof: [Sourced cost comparison from switchers, with n and date]
```

### Objection Handling

**Common Objections & Responses**

Format each as:
```
**Objection:** "[Exact words customers say]"
**Response:** [Your counter]
**Redirect:** [How to refocus on your strengths]
```

**Price Objections**
```
Objection: "They're cheaper than you"
Response: "Let's look at total cost. Many customers find they overpay with [Competitor] due to [hidden fees/overages/unused features]. [Cite your sourced total-cost comparison here, with n and date.]"
Redirect: "What matters most - lowest sticker price or best value for what you actually need?"
```

**Feature Objections**
```
Objection: "They have [specific feature] you don't"
Response: "You're right, we approach this differently. Here's why..." [Explain superior approach]
Redirect: "What problem are you trying to solve with that feature? Let me show you how we solve that."
```

**"We're already using them" Objections**
```
Objection: "We're already using [Competitor]"
Response: "That's great that you've already solved for [category]. What's working well? What would you change if you could?"
Redirect: [Listen for dissatisfaction] "That's exactly what we've heard from other customers who switched from [Competitor]..."
```

**Competitive Objections**
```
Objection: "They're bigger/more established"
Response: "That's true - and many customers found that meant [slower innovation/less responsive support/one-size-fits-all]. We focus on [your differentiator]."
Redirect: "What's more important - brand name recognition or [your unique value]?"
```

### Sales Plays

**Discovery Questions to Ask**
Questions that naturally lead to your strengths

*Categories:*
- Current state assessment
- Pain point identification
- Decision criteria exploration
- Competitor satisfaction probing

*Example Set:*
```
Current State:
"Walk me through how you handle [the job your product does] today?"

Pain Point:
"What's the biggest challenge you face with your current setup?"

Decision Criteria:
"What would make you consider switching providers?"

Competitor Probing:
"How responsive is your current provider when you need support?"
```

**Proof Points Specific to This Competitor**
Data and stories relevant to this competitive situation

*Include:*
- Win stories (customers who switched from them)
- Performance comparisons (if available)
- Customer quotes about switching
- ROI data from switchers

*Example:*
"[Customer] switched from [Competitor] and [specific, sourced outcome with a number and a timeframe] by [the change that caused it]."

**References and Case Studies**
Customers who switched from this competitor

*For Each:*
- Customer profile (industry, size, use case)
- Why they switched
- Results achieved
- Quote (if available)
- Contact info (if willing reference)

## Competitive Positioning by Competitor Type

### Platform Competitors (All-in-One Tools)

**Positioning Strategy:** Cost & Service

**Key Battles:**
- Pricing transparency and value
- Customer support quality
- Ease of use vs. complexity
- Feature utilization

**Winning Themes:**
- "Usable sophistication" - features that actually work
- "Right-sized support" - not tiered by account size
- "Transparent value" - no surprise price hikes

**Trap-Setting Questions:**
- "How much of [Platform's] functionality do you actually use?"
- "How often do you get the support you need when you need it?"
- "What was your experience with their last price increase?"

### Point Solution Competitors (Single-Job Tools)

**Positioning Strategy:** Consolidation & Efficiency

**Key Battles:**
- Platform fragmentation
- Data silos and attribution
- Login/workflow complexity
- Total cost of ownership

**Winning Themes:**
- "Unified platform" - everything in one place
- "Clear visibility" - know what's working
- "Simplified workflow" - no swivel-chair work

**Trap-Setting Questions:**
- "How much time do you spend switching between platforms?"
- "Can you see the full picture without exporting to a spreadsheet?"
- "What's your total spend across all the tools involved in this workflow?"

## Switcher-Specific Strategies

### Understanding Switcher Segments

**Platform Switchers**
*Profile:* Came from an all-in-one suite that covers this category among many others
*Top Needs:* Cost certainty, accessible support, usable sophistication
*Pain Points:* Rising costs, poor support for smaller accounts, feature complexity

**Point Solution Switchers**
*Profile:* Came from a standalone point tool for this one job
*Top Needs:* Unified management, clear visibility, transparent billing
*Pain Points:* "Swivel-chairing," murky ROI, confusing pricing

### Switcher Battlecard Enhancements

**For Platform Switchers:**
Add sections on:
- Cost comparison calculators
- Support tier differences
- Implementation complexity comparison
- Feature utilization analysis

**For Point Solution Switchers:**
Add sections on:
- Workflow consolidation benefits
- Attribution clarity examples
- Pricing transparency comparison
- Integration elimination

## Building a Competitive Battlecard

### Step 1: Intelligence Gathering

**Sources:**
- Win/loss interviews (most valuable)
- Sales call recordings and feedback
- Customer research and surveys
- Product/feature evaluation
- Competitor marketing materials
- Third-party reviews (G2, Capterra, etc.)
- Industry analyst reports

**What to Capture:**
- Competitor positioning and messaging
- Pricing and packaging model
- Key features and capabilities
- Target customers and verticals
- Strengths and weaknesses (honest assessment)
- Customer satisfaction themes
- Recent product updates or changes

### Step 2: Analysis & Synthesis

**Identify Patterns:**
- Where do we consistently win?
- Where do we lose?
- What do customers say about them?
- What pain points surface repeatedly?
- What objections come up most often?

**Determine Positioning:**
- What's our #1 differentiator vs. them?
- What customer segment do we serve better?
- What use cases are we stronger for?
- What's our compelling event to switch?

### Step 3: Content Creation

**Write for Sellers:**
- Lead with what matters most (quick ref at top)
- Use bullet points, not paragraphs
- Include specific language sellers can use
- Provide exact questions to ask
- Give proof points with sources

**Validate with Sales:**
- Does this match what they're hearing?
- Is this useful in real conversations?
- What's missing that they need?
- Is the language natural and conversational?

### Step 4: Enablement & Distribution

**Make It Accessible:**
- Publish wherever sellers already look (enablement platform, CRM, team wiki)
- Create both detailed and quick-ref versions
- Build into CRM for easy access
- Include in new hire onboarding

**Train the Team:**
- Live training on positioning strategy
- Role-play objection handling
- Practice trap-setting questions
- Share win stories

### Step 5: Maintenance & Updates

**Regular Updates:**
- Quarterly review at minimum
- Immediate updates for major competitive changes
- Continuous feedback loop from sales
- Win/loss integration

**Track Effectiveness:**
- Win rate against this competitor
- Usage metrics (views, downloads)
- Sales feedback on usefulness
- Objection resolution rates

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Feature Parity Lists**
❌ 50-row feature comparison spreadsheet
✅ 3-5 meaningful differentiators that matter to customers

**Competitor Bashing**
❌ "They're terrible and everyone hates them"
✅ "Here's where they're strong, and here's where we excel"

**Stale Information**
❌ Battlecard from 2 years ago
✅ Updated quarterly with fresh win/loss insights

**Too Much Detail**
❌ 10-page competitive analysis
✅ 1-2 page scannable battlecard

**Generic Positioning**
❌ "We're better quality"
✅ "We offer unified platform vs. their point solution, eliminating swivel-chair work"

**No Proof Points**
❌ "Customers love us"
✅ "[Outcome metric] improved [X%] for customers who [did the thing] (n=[sample], [quarter/year])"

## Best Practices

**Be Honest**
Acknowledge where competitors are genuinely strong. Sales teams trust battlecards that are balanced.

**Focus on Outcomes**
Don't just list features. Explain why the difference matters to the customer's business.

**Use Customer Language**
Pull quotes directly from win/loss interviews. Real customer words are more powerful than marketing speak.

**Make It Scannable**
Sales teams don't read paragraphs mid-call. Use bullets, tables, and clear headers.

**Provide Exact Questions**
Don't just say "ask about pricing." Give the exact question: "Walk me through your monthly bill from [Competitor]."

**Update Continuously**
Set calendar reminders for quarterly reviews. Make it someone's job to keep these fresh.

**Celebrate Wins**
Share success stories where battlecards helped close deals. This reinforces usage.
